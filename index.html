About Me

I am a research fellow in the Solar and Magnetospheric Theory Group in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at the University of St Andrews, where I study magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) waves in the solar corona. I use numerical simulations to model how waves travel through large-scale coronal structures such as helmet streamers, and compare the results with observations from instruments such as the Metis coronagraph on Solar Orbiter.

Before this I was a research fellow in the School of Physics and Astronomy at St Andrews (2021–2025), working on stellar prominences and coronal rain: how cool, dense material condenses in the magnetic fields of stars, and what happens when it is ejected or falls back to the stellar surface.

I graduated from the University of York in 2014 with a first-class MPhys in Theoretical Physics, and completed my PhD in astrophysics in 2018. I then held postdoctoral positions at Maison de la Simulation, CEA Saclay, Paris, simulating convection in exoplanet atmospheres, and at the Cavendish Laboratory, University of Cambridge, developing software for the Square Kilometre Array.
